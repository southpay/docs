Build a product that moves or holds money and, usually sooner than you planned, you reach questions it cannot answer on its own. Why is this balance what it is. Which entry changed it. What happened between two dates. Does it reconcile with your processor and your bank. These questions do not show up because you adopted a ledger. They show up because you are building this class of product.

A correct, auditable, double-entry ledger is the thing that answers them. You will need one. The only real decision is whether you build and maintain it yourself or adopt it as a primitive. So this page is less about how we compare and more about the need you are heading toward, and where each common path leaves you when you get there.

Southpay Ledger is a developer ledger API: primitives (accounts, postings, transactions, balances), you write the business logic, and the platform guarantees the [invariants](/ledger/invariants). It is self-serve and API-first. You sign up, get OAuth client credentials, and ship without a sales call.

## You start on Postgres

Almost everyone does, and it works until it doesn't. The first retried request that double-posts, the first balance that is off by a cent, the first reconciliation that will not tie out: each one sends you back to rebuild an invariant you did not know you needed yet. Balanced-per-currency enforcement, append-only history, idempotency, atomic multi-posting writes, derived balances that stay fast, multi-currency, point-in-time history, reconciliation. You end up building the ledger anyway, one incident at a time, in production, with real money on it.

Southpay Ledger is that correctness layer, already built and enforced. The full argument, including where Postgres genuinely is the right tool (we run on it), is on the [why not just use Postgres?](/ledger/why-not-postgres) page.

## A processor moves the money, not the meaning

A payment processor like Stripe authorizes, captures, and settles payments across the card networks and bank rails. It is very good at moving money. It never becomes your system of record for what that money meant.

The processor tells you a charge succeeded. It does not tell you which accounts that charge affected, in what currency, or what your balances are as a result. That is the ledger's job, and you still need it. A common setup is to take payments through a processor and write the corresponding transactions into Southpay Ledger as the record of truth.

<Info>
  Southpay Ledger is not a payment processor. It does not touch card or bank rails. It records and proves the accounting behind whatever moved the money.
</Info>

## Accounting software answers your finance team, not your product

Accounting software like QuickBooks or Xero maintains your company's books, produces statements for tax and compliance, and is operated by humans through a UI on a periodic close cycle. It answers your finance team.

Your product asks different questions, at a different speed. It needs a programmable system of record it writes to in real time over an API, as transactions happen, at the granularity of your application's own accounts. That is a developer ledger. Many companies run both, with the ledger feeding summarized figures into their accounting system.

## Once you accept you need a ledger API

Fragment.dev and similar developer ledger APIs sit in the same category as Southpay Ledger. If you are weighing them, you have already arrived at the need this whole page is about. From here it is a question of approach, not category.

Southpay Ledger is developer-first: API keys in minutes, no sales call, primitives over opinions. The platform stays neutral, so it bakes in no domain-specific vocabulary, and it makes its guarantees explicit rather than implicit (see the [invariants](/ledger/invariants) page). If you compare us against another ledger API, compare on exactly that: how fast you get keys, how few assumptions the primitives make about your domain, and how clearly the invariants are stated and enforced.

## Is this for you?

**Yes, if you are building a product that needs an auditable money system of record.** A treasury startup, a marketplace splitting funds across parties, an FX desk, a SaaS company billing its own usage. You want the ledger invariants guaranteed while you write the business logic on top. You would otherwise build this yourself, and you would rather not.

**No, if you only need bookkeeping for your own company's taxes and statements.** That is what accounting software is for. Reach for QuickBooks or Xero. You do not need a programmable ledger to close your own books.
